# Posting to Performing Scorecard: campaign kit

Everything around the asset. Landing page copy, the lead routing rules, the
follow-up sequence, and the promotion that feeds it.

House voice throughout: confident, warm, specific. No exclamation points, no em
dashes, no influencer fluff, no agency stiffness.

---

## 1. Lead routing

AGENCY818 engagements begin at **$4,000 a month**. That number is the filter, so
the scorecard asks for it directly rather than guessing at fit.

The scorecard returns two independent readings. Use both.

- **Need** comes from the score. Low score means large gap.
- **Fit** comes from the investment band and the timeline.

Need without fit is a newsletter subscriber. Fit without need is a hard sell.
The money is in the middle.

| Segment | Score | Investment | Timeline | Route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Book now** | 25 to 50 | $4k+ | Ready now or this quarter | Call invitation on screen, personal reply within 24 hours |
| **B. Stretch** | 25 to 50 | $2k to $4k | Ready now or this quarter | Full sequence. They are one step below the price. Make the case for the gap being worth $4k before quoting it |
| **C. Nurture** | any | any | Next quarter | Full sequence, no call push, re-invite at 60 days |
| **D. List** | any | Under $2k | Exploring | Email 1 only, then the general list |

Segment B is where the price gets tested. Do not discount into it. A brand at
$3k a month is usually paying for output, and the scorecard has just shown them
which pillar that output is failing. That gap is the argument for $4k, and it is
a better argument than a discount.

The best lead in this system is not the lowest score. It is a brand scoring
**25 to 50 that already invests $4k or more**: they take content seriously, it
is underperforming, and they can tell the difference. A 12 out of 60 with no
budget is a long education, not a client.

Segment A gets a human reply, not an automation. Reference their weakest pillar
by name in the first line.

### CRM properties to create

`scorecard_total`, `scorecard_tier`, `scorecard_weakest_pillar`,
`pillar_pov`, `pillar_visual`, `pillar_engine`, `pillar_path`, `pillar_signal`,
`content_investment`, `content_produced_by`, `scorecard_date`.

Re-scoring at 90 days is the retention hook. Movement in a pillar is the
easiest case study you will ever write.

## 2. Landing page copy

**Eyebrow**
Twenty questions. Five pillars.

**Headline**
Find out whether your content is posting or performing.

**Subhead**
Most brands do not have a content problem. They have a direction problem that
shows up as content. This scorecard grades the five things that decide whether
a feed builds a business or just fills a calendar, and tells you which one is
costing you the most right now.

**What you get**
- A score out of 60, broken down by pillar
- Your weakest pillar named, with the three moves we would run first
- A written breakdown of all five, sent to your inbox

**Who it is for**
Beauty, wellness, and fashion brands already spending real money on content and
not seeing it return. If you are looking for a posting schedule template, this
is not that.

**Objection block**
*Six minutes is a lot for a quiz.*
It is. That is deliberate. Four questions per pillar is the minimum needed to
tell a system problem from a bad month, and a score you can act on is worth six
minutes more than a score you cannot.

**Closing CTA**
Score your content. Answer honestly. A flattering score is worth nothing.

## 3. Follow-up sequence

Five emails. Merge fields in braces come from the payload.

### Email 1 — immediately

**Subject:** Your score: {score} out of 60
**Preview:** And the pillar that is costing you the most.

{firstName},

Here is your breakdown.

You scored {score} out of 60, which puts {brand} in **{tier}**.

Your weakest pillar is **{weakestPillar}** at {weakestScore} out of 12. That is
where the return is, and it is worth being specific about why: [one paragraph
written per pillar, kept on file, dropped in by merge].

Three moves, in order:

1. [move one]
2. [move two]
3. [move three]

Work those for thirty days and retake the scorecard. If the number does not
move, the problem is a layer below where you are looking, and that is worth a
conversation.

— Aly

### Email 2 — day 3

**Subject:** The pillar nobody scores well on

Across the brands who take this, {weakestPillar} is rarely the surprise. The
surprise is Conversion Path.

Beautiful feeds that end in nothing. A link in bio to a homepage. DMs with
buying intent sitting unanswered for two days.

Here is the fix in its smallest form: pick one offer, build one page for it,
and make every third post point at that page specifically. Not the brand.
The offer.

[Short case detail, one brand, one number, no hype.]

### Email 3 — day 6

**Subject:** What we would do with {brand} in the first thirty days

Not a pitch. A plan, based on what you told us.

Week one: [tied to weakest pillar]
Week two: [production]
Weeks three and four: [conversion path]

That is the shape of a Content Intensive. If you want it walked through against
your actual feed, thirty minutes is here: [booking link].

*Segment B: keep the plan, move the price to the call rather than the email.*
*Segments C and D: cut the last paragraph. Replace with an invitation to reply.*

### Email 4 — day 10

**Subject:** Strategy meets storytelling

Why we work the way we do. The founder's story, the on-camera angle, what an
actress-turned-strategist notices about your content that a media buyer does
not. One proof point. One invitation.

### Email 5 — day 21

**Subject:** Retake it

Thirty days on. Has anything moved?

The scorecard is here again: [link]. Same twenty questions. If your number went
up, tell us which move did it. If it did not, tell us where you got stuck and
we will point you at the next thing.

## 4. Promotion

The scorecard only works if the right people see it. Six posts, each earning
the click a different way.

**1. Carousel, the five pillars**
Slide one: "Your content is not underperforming. One of these five is."
One slide per pillar, named, with the failure state written plainly.
Final slide: score yourself, link in bio.

**2. Reel, founder to camera, 30 seconds**
"I can tell in about eight seconds whether a brand's content is going to make
them money, and it has nothing to do with how pretty it is." Three tells, then
the scorecard.

**3. Single image, the hard question**
"Cover your logo. Would anyone know it was you?" One line of body copy. Link.

**4. Story sequence, three frames**
Frame one: poll, "Do you know your best performing post of last quarter and
why it worked?" Frame two: the results, no commentary. Frame three: link.

**5. Reel, the score reveal format**
Screen recording of the scorecard being taken on a real brand, score revealed
at the end. Ask permission, or use your own.

**6. Carousel, the middle-score post**
"The most expensive score on this scorecard is not the low one." Explain why
brands in the 39 to 50 range are burning the most money. Speaks directly to
segment A.

### DM script for the reply to a scorecard share

Someone posts their score or replies to a story.

> {name}, {score} is a real number. The pillar to look at is {weakestPillar},
> and the fastest thing you can do this week is [one specific move]. Want me to
> look at the feed and tell you what I would cut first?

No pitch in the first message. The offer to look is the offer.

## 5. What to measure

| Metric | Where the problem is if it is low |
|---|---|
| Start rate | The headline or the audience |
| Completion rate | Question length or option clarity |
| Results to form rate | The results page is giving away too much or too little |
| Segment A share | Wrong traffic, not a broken funnel |
| Reply rate to email 1 | The per-pillar paragraphs are too generic |

Completion rate under 55 percent means the questions are doing the filtering
too hard. Above 85 percent means they are not filtering at all.
